\begin{abstract}
This manuscript studies conservative transfer of edge-normal flux degrees of
freedom between nonmatching polygonal meshes in the plane and on the unit
sphere.  The method follows the mimetic polygon reconstruction of Perot and
Chartrand \cite{PerotChartrand2021}: in each source cell, signed edge fluxes
determine a local Raviart--Thomas-like vector field with constant cell
divergence and a harmonic gradient component.  Target edge fluxes are then
obtained by clipping each target edge against source cells and integrating the
source reconstruction on the clipped subsegments.  The manuscript also analyzes
an alternative based on scalar Wachspress coordinates.  We show that a
componentwise vertex-based Wachspress interpolant is not a direct substitute for
the harmonic mimetic reconstruction because its divergence and edge-normal trace
are generically nonconstant on \(n\)-gons.  Generalized barycentric and virtual
element H(div) constructions remain plausible future research directions, but
they require a different vector-valued space and validation program rather than
a basis swap.  The study also introduces a unified higher-order polygonal
\(H(\mathrm{div})\)-style split-moment family with user-controlled edge moment
order, a local decomposition into polynomial-divergence, harmonic-gradient, and
divergence-free completion modes, exact hard enforcement of the zeroth edge
flux moment, and weighted higher-moment fitting.  Numerical evidence from
deterministic planar and spherical structured and unstructured tests verifies
conservation, direct edge transfer, and sparse projection equivalence to a
\(5\times 10^{-13}\) absolute tolerance.  On refined planar quad patches, the
high-order extension shows average edge-flux convergence rates of approximately
\(2.26/3.39/4.46\) for \(p=1,2,3\); on refined planar Voronoi patches, the
harmonic \(p=1\) baseline and split-moment \(p=2,3\) enrichments yield average
rates \(1.89/2.77/3.34\).  The same split-moment hierarchy extends to
structured cubed-sphere and deterministic spherical Voronoi-patch transfers,
where a degree-elevated basis captures the leading Piola rational correction
and yields zeroth-moment convergence rates of approximately
\(1.75/3.08/4.57\) (cubed-sphere) and \(2.05/2.52/3.62\) (Voronoi patches)
for \(p=1,2,3\), with machine-precision target-cell closure throughout.
The conforming-projection step is also generalized to a block-coupled
multi-degree formulation that exposes the entire moment vector as a
single unknown over the geometric target skeleton with proper
edge-mass-weighted L\(^2\) Legendre objective, and exposes a
source-skeleton trace-jump diagnostic that refines as \(O(h^{p+1})\)
and serves as the asymptotic indicator of source-reconstruction
continuity.  A practical single-flux production pipeline (one scalar
per source edge \(\to\) patch-based moment recovery \(\to\) VEM
projection) is verified to retain the gradient-subspace asymptotic
rates without any high-order seed data.  A Hodge-weighted spherical
lift of the VEM path is implemented and verified to deliver
\(O(h^{p+1})\) convergence in the gradient subspace at \(p=1\) on
cubed-sphere meshes; the rotational subspace currently sits at
\(O(h^2)\) by a centroid-Hodge approximation and is the open task
of the spherical VEM lift.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The accurate numerical representation of vector-valued fields obeying
conservation laws lies at the heart of computational physics and geophysical
modeling.  The mathematical framework underpinning these representations is
the \emph{de Rham complex}, which in two dimensions reads
\[
  H^1(\Omega) \xrightarrow{\nabla} H(\mathrm{div};\Omega)
             \xrightarrow{\nabla\cdot} L^2(\Omega).
\]
Edge-normal fluxes belong naturally to $H(\mathrm{div})$-conforming spaces
whose degrees of freedom are attached to codimension-one entities.  Discrete
counterparts of this complex -- \emph{mimetic} or \emph{structure-preserving}
schemes -- maintain the key differential-geometric properties at the discrete
level, avoiding spurious modes and preserving local conservation
\cite{BochevHyman2006,Mattiussi1997,ArnoldFalkWinther2006}.

Conservative interpolation between nonmatching polygon meshes is normally
formulated for cell averages, where a source-target overlap mesh defines
weights that preserve the integral of a scalar conserved quantity
\cite{DukowiczKodis1987,Jones1999,UllrichTaylor2015}.  The problem considered
here is different: the data are signed normal flux integrals on source-cell
edges, and the desired output is the corresponding signed normal flux integral
on each directed target-cell edge.  This is an $H(\mathrm{div})$-type transfer
problem.  It must preserve flux balance around every target cell, but it must
also give edge-wise degrees of freedom suitable for compatible finite volume or
mixed finite element discretizations.

The central design requirement is local conservation on arbitrary source-target
intersections.  If \(O\) is a clipped subregion of a source cell \(K\), the
reconstructed source field must satisfy
\[
  \int_{\partial O}\uh\cdot\n\,ds = \int_O \nabla\cdot\uh\,dA
\]
with the right-hand side represented consistently from the source edge fluxes.
The lowest-order compatible case is attractive because
$\nabla\cdot\uh=d_K$ is constant in each source cell, reducing the overlap
invariant to $d_K |O|$ independently of the clipped polygon shape.  This
constant-divergence structure is the mathematical reason \emph{harmonic
polynomial bases} -- whose gradients are automatically divergence-free via
$\nabla\cdot(\nabla p) = \Delta p = 0$ -- are a natural fit for conservative
edge-flux transfer.  The method follows the Perot--Chartrand construction
\cite{PerotChartrand2021}: in each source cell, signed edge fluxes determine a
local Raviart--Thomas-like vector field with constant cell divergence and a
harmonic gradient component.

The study extends this baseline in several directions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a spherical extension using source-cell gnomonic charts, great-circle
        edge clipping, and a contravariant Piola flux map that preserves normal
        fluxes between chart and surface curves;
  \item a unified higher-order polygonal $H(\mathrm{div})$-style split-moment
        family with a local decomposition into polynomial-divergence,
        harmonic-gradient, and divergence-free completion modes, exact hard
        conservation of the zeroth edge moment, and weighted higher-moment
        fitting for $p=1,2,3$;
  \item metric-corrected basis orthogonalization and degree-elevated
        polynomial enrichment for high-order spherical convergence;
  \item a Piola-consistent RT backward reconstruction eliminating ill-conditioning on quad cells,
        with insufficient edge constraints;
  \item a topology-independent VEM H(div) projection using the decomposed
        basis $\nabla P_{p+1}\oplus\mathbf{x}^{\perp}P_{p-1}$ with
        well-conditioned mass matrix on arbitrary polygons (including
        triangles, hexagons, and irregular n-gons), and a Hodge-weighted
        spherical lift of the same path;
  \item a patch-based moment recovery from single-flux-per-edge data
        that synthesizes the missing high-order edge and cell moments
        for downstream VEM projection, enabling production-style
        single-flux remap pipelines without any upstream high-order
        discretization;
  \item a coupled multi-degree H(div)-conforming target-skeleton
        projection with edge-mass-weighted L\(^2\) Legendre objective and
        a source-skeleton trace-jump diagnostic that refines as
        \(O(h^{p+1})\) and is the asymptotic indicator of
        source-reconstruction continuity;
  \item nanoflann k-d tree spatial acceleration and OpenMP parallelization
        of the source-cell search; and
  \item verification on rectangular, planar Voronoi, cubed-sphere,
        icosahedral dual Voronoi, and lat/lon nonmatching patches, including
        full-sphere round-trip transfer in equirectangular projection and
        a forward-only diagnostic that separates source-side reconstruction
        quality from backward-leg amplification.
\end{itemize}

\section{Mathematical Method}

\subsection{Input and Output Data}

Let \(K\) be a source polygon with boundary edges \(f \subset \partial K\).
The discrete source field is the signed integrated normal flux
\begin{equation}
  U_f = \int_f \mathbf{u}\cdot\n_f\, ds,
  \label{eq:edge_flux}
\end{equation}
where \(\n_f\) is the outward unit normal of \(K\).  The output on a target edge
or an overlap boundary edge is another signed integral of the reconstructed
field.  Conservative interpolation is judged by exact agreement of integrated
fluxes on source-target intersections, not by pointwise vector agreement.

\subsection{Level-2 Harmonic Reconstruction}

In two dimensions the Perot-Chartrand reconstruction writes the cell field as
\begin{equation}
  \uh(\x) = \nabla\phi(\x) + \frac{d}{2}\x,
  \label{eq:pc_reconstruction}
\end{equation}
where \(d\) is constant in the cell and \(\phi\) is harmonic.  We use a level-2
truncated harmonic polynomial basis,
\begin{align}
  P_1 &= x, & Q_1 &= y,\\
  P_2 &= x^2-y^2, & Q_2 &= 2xy,
  \label{eq:basis}
\end{align}
so that
\begin{equation}
  \uh(\x)
    = \frac{d}{2}\x
      + a_1\nabla P_1 + b_1\nabla Q_1
      + a_2\nabla P_2 + b_2\nabla Q_2.
  \label{eq:level2}
\end{equation}
All coordinates are expressed in a centroid-relative local planar
frame.  This reduces low-order moment coupling and matches the assumptions used
in the paper's construction.

The constant divergence is fixed by the source fluxes:
\begin{equation}
  d = \frac{1}{|K|}\sum_{f\subset\partial K} U_f.
  \label{eq:divergence}
\end{equation}
For any scalar test function \(H\), Gauss' theorem gives
\begin{equation}
  \int_K \nabla H\cdot \mathbf{u}\,dA
  =
  \sum_{f\subset\partial K} U_f
  \left(\overline{H}_f-\overline{H}_K\right),
  \label{eq:moment}
\end{equation}
under the lowest-order assumption that the edge-normal data are constant per
edge.  The known divergence contribution \(\frac{d}{2}\x\) is subtracted before
solving the small harmonic system
\begin{equation}
  V\alpha = r,\qquad
  \alpha = (a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2)^T,
  \label{eq:local_system}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  V_{ij} = \int_K \nabla H_i\cdot\nabla H_j\,dA
  \label{eq:gram}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
  r_i =
    \sum_{f\subset\partial K} U_f
      \left(\overline{H_i}_f-\overline{H_i}_K\right)
    - \frac{d}{2}\int_K \x\cdot\nabla H_i\,dA.
  \label{eq:rhs}
\end{equation}
Here \(H_i\in\{P_1,Q_1,P_2,Q_2\}\).  Since the current truncation has four
harmonic unknowns, the local solve cost is constant per cell.

\subsection{Reduction and Conservative Overlap Integrals}

Because \(\uh\) is a gradient-form field plus \(\frac{d}{2}\x\), a line
integral from \(A\) to \(B\) can be evaluated exactly:
\begin{equation}
  \int_A^B \uh\cdot d\x
  =
  \frac{d}{4}\left(|B|^2-|A|^2\right)
  + \sum_i a_i\left(P_i(B)-P_i(A)\right)
  + \sum_i b_i\left(Q_i(B)-Q_i(A)\right).
  \label{eq:line_integral}
\end{equation}
For an overlap polygon \(O=K_s\cap K_t\), the conservative property is tested by
integrating the outward-normal flux around the overlap boundary:
\begin{equation}
  \int_{\partial O} {\uh}_s\cdot\n\,ds = d_s |O|.
  \label{eq:overlap_conservation}
\end{equation}
This is the local divergence theorem.  It is the key invariant that a
conservative source-to-target interpolation must satisfy on every intersection
region.

\subsection{Edge-wise Source-to-Target Transfer}

The overlap diagnostic above is necessary but not sufficient for a usable edge
remap.  A target edge degree of freedom must be computed on the target edge
itself.  For a directed target edge \(e_t=[A,B]\) with target outward normal
\(\n_t\), the implemented transfer clips the edge against every source cell and
sums the source-cell contributions:
\begin{equation}
  U^t_{e_t}
  =
  \sum_{K_s:\, e_t\cap K_s\neq\emptyset}
  \int_{e_t\cap K_s} {\uh}_{K_s}\cdot\n_t\,ds .
  \label{eq:edge_transfer}
\end{equation}
The segment orientation is inherited from the target cell boundary, so the
normal in Eq.~\eqref{eq:edge_transfer} is the target-cell normal rather than the
source-cell normal.  This is the edge-wise source-to-target transfer that the
current nonmatching tests now validate.

The map is linear in the source edge fluxes.  For each source cell \(K_s\), the
local reconstruction can be written
\begin{equation}
  \beta_{K_s}=B_{K_s}U_{K_s},
  \qquad
  \beta_{K_s}=(d,a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2)^T,
  \label{eq:local_reconstruction_matrix}
\end{equation}
and each clipped target-edge segment defines a row functional
\(L_{\gamma,K_s}\).  The assembled sparse projection is therefore
\begin{equation}
  U_t = P U_s,\qquad
  P_{e_t,f_s} \mathrel{+}=
  L_{\gamma,K_s} B_{K_s}(:,f_s).
  \label{eq:sparse_projection}
\end{equation}
Rows of \(P\) are directed target-cell edges, and columns are directed
source-cell edges.  This directed convention is important because normal flux
orientation is cell-local; a shared undirected mesh edge needs a separate
orientation map before edge DOFs are collapsed.

\subsection{Spherical Gnomonic Backend}

The spherical backend is a geometry-correct extension of the planar algorithm
for unit-sphere great-circle polygons.  For each source cell \(K_s\), the method
constructs an orthonormal frame
\[
  \{\mathbf{e}_1,\mathbf{e}_2,\mathbf{c}\},
\]
where \(\mathbf{c}\) is the normalized cell-center direction and
\(\mathbf{e}_1,\mathbf{e}_2\) span the tangent plane.  A point
\(\mathbf{r}\in S^2\) in the visible hemisphere is projected to chart
coordinates by
\begin{equation}
  \xi(\mathbf{r}) =
  \left(
  \frac{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{e}_1}{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{c}},
  \frac{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{e}_2}{\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{c}}
  \right).
  \label{eq:gnomonic}
\end{equation}
The inverse chart is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{r}(\xi,\eta)
  =
  \frac{\mathbf{c}+\xi\mathbf{e}_1+\eta\mathbf{e}_2}
       {\|\mathbf{c}+\xi\mathbf{e}_1+\eta\mathbf{e}_2\|}.
  \label{eq:inverse_gnomonic}
\end{equation}
Gnomonic projection maps great circles to straight lines, so a directed target
great-circle edge becomes a straight segment in every source-cell chart where it
is visible.  The spherical transfer therefore clips that projected segment
against the projected source polygon and evaluates the same line functional used
in the planar algorithm.

Let \(J=\partial\mathbf{r}/\partial(\xi,\eta)\) and let
\(|J|=\|\partial_\xi\mathbf{r}\times\partial_\eta\mathbf{r}\|\).  The
contravariant Piola lift from chart vector \(\mathbf{w}\) to surface tangent
vector \(\mathbf{u}\) is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{u} = \frac{J\mathbf{w}}{|J|}.
  \label{eq:piola_lift}
\end{equation}
The inverse map used by the manufactured-field tests is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{w} =
  |J|\,(J^T J)^{-1}J^T\mathbf{u}.
  \label{eq:piola_pullback}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:piola_lift}--\eqref{eq:piola_pullback} preserve normal
fluxes across corresponding chart and surface curves.  They also imply the
surface-chart identity
\begin{equation}
  \int_{\Gamma_K} \nabla_\Gamma \psi \cdot \mathbf{u}\, dA
  =
  \int_{\widehat K} \nabla_{\xi}\widehat\psi \cdot \mathbf{w}\, d\xi,
  \qquad
  \widehat\psi = \psi \circ \mathbf{r},
  \label{eq:piola_moment_identity}
\end{equation}
so the flux moments and the divergence relation remain chart-based.  The
surface \(L^2\) inner product of two lifted chart fields
\(\mathbf{u}_i = J\mathbf{w}_i/|J|\) and \(\mathbf{u}_j = J\mathbf{w}_j/|J|\)
is, however,
\begin{equation}
  \int_{\Gamma_K} \mathbf{u}_i \cdot \mathbf{u}_j\, dA
  =
  \int_{\widehat K} \mathbf{w}_i^T
  \left(\frac{J^T J}{|J|}\right)
  \mathbf{w}_j\, d\xi.
  \label{eq:piola_hodge}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the spherical reconstruction keeps the right-hand side moments
from Eq.~\eqref{eq:rhs} in chart measure, but replaces the planar Gram matrix
with the Hodge/Piola-consistent tensor \(J^T J / |J|\).  A scalar area-scale
factor alone is not sufficient for this vector inner product because the metric
couples chart components through \(J^T J\).

The spherical direct transfer and sparse projection use the identical clipped
segment functional.  A target edge that lies exactly on a source edge can be
accepted by two adjacent source cells under naive all-pairs clipping; the method
resolves this deterministically by retaining the source cell on the
target-cell-interior side of the directed target edge.  This tie break is
essential for cubed-sphere \(N\to N\) identity tests and for reproducible sparse
operator assembly.


\subsection{Constrained Reconstruction Solve}
The reconstruction is computed as a constrained local least-squares problem:
edge fluxes are enforced as hard constraints, while the remaining degrees of
freedom are selected by the mimetic energy inner product.  This avoids the
failure mode of an unconstrained minimum-norm fit, which can preserve global
balance while losing the moment consistency needed for convergence.  The
resulting Karush--Kuhn--Tucker (KKT) system is
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}
V & C^T \\
C & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
X \\
L
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
M \\
F
\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
Here, \(V\) is the harmonic-basis mass matrix, \(C\) is the edge-integral
constraint matrix, \(M\) is the Galerkin moment vector, \(F\) is the vector of
input edge fluxes, \(X\) contains the reconstruction coefficients, and \(L\) are
the Lagrange multipliers enforcing exact flux recovery.

For highly irregular polygons, high-degree modes can be poorly conditioned.  A
Tikhonov penalty can be applied to selected high-mode diagonal entries of \(V\)
without weakening the hard constraints \(CX=F\):
\begin{equation}
V_{ii} \leftarrow V_{ii} + 1.0 \times 10^2 |A_K| \quad \text{for } i \ge 4
\end{equation}
where \(|A_K|\) is the polygon area.  The penalty is not part of the
conservation proof; it is a conditioning safeguard for higher modes.

\subsection{Why the Raw Nonmatching Transfer Can Lose Target-Cell Closure}

The local reconstruction on each source cell is RT-like in the sense that its
divergence is constant:
\[
  \nabla\cdot {\uh}_s = d_s .
\]
Therefore, if the source edge fluxes close on \(K_s\), then the source-cell
reconstruction is exactly divergence-free whenever \(d_s=0\).  This does
\emph{not} imply that the \emph{raw transferred target edges} close on every
nonmatching target cell.  The distinction is essential.

Let \(K_t\) be one target cell and let \(\Gamma_s^\circ\) denote the union of
interior source-cell interfaces.  Summing the overlap balances over all source
cells that intersect \(K_t\) gives
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{e\subset \partial K_t} U^{\mathrm{raw}}_e
  =
  \sum_{K_s} d_s |K_t\cap K_s|
  -
  \sum_{\gamma \subset K_t \cap \Gamma_s^\circ}
  \int_\gamma \bigl[\!\bigl[ \uh \cdot \n_\gamma \bigr]\!\bigr] \, ds .
  \label{eq:target_balance_with_jumps}
\end{equation}
The first term is the desired target-cell divergence integral inherited from the
source reconstructions.  The second term is a jump contribution across interior
source interfaces.  It vanishes only when adjacent source-cell reconstructions
induce the same normal trace on their shared edge.

This explains the behavior seen in the divergence-free Voronoi figures.  Each
source reconstruction can satisfy \(d_s=0\) exactly, yet the target-cell edge
sum can still be nonzero if neighboring source cells agree only on the
\emph{integrated} source-edge flux and not on the full pointwise normal trace.
Conversely, the quad-to-quad harmonic-exact case is nearly exact because the
manufactured field lies in the reproduced space, so adjacent source-cell
reconstructions collapse to the same global field and the jump term in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_balance_with_jumps} disappears to roundoff.

The same mechanism can be monitored directly on the target skeleton.  For one
interior geometric target edge \(g\) shared by two target cells \(K_t^-\) and
\(K_t^+\), define the raw edge-jump defect
\begin{equation}
  J_g^{\mathrm{raw}}
  =
  U_{g,-}^{\mathrm{raw}} + U_{g,+}^{\mathrm{raw}},
  \label{eq:raw-edge-jump}
\end{equation}
where both edge fluxes are oriented with the outward normals of their
respective target cells.  A discrete \(H(\mathrm{div})\)-conforming target edge
field satisfies \(J_g=0\) on every interior edge because the two outward
normals are opposite.  In the present implementation, once coincident target
vertices are merged, the raw transfer already satisfies \(J_g^{\mathrm{raw}}=0\)
to roundoff because each shared target edge is integrated geometrically and then
seen from two opposite target-cell orientations.  This is an important
distinction: the nonzero target-cell divergence residual in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_balance_with_jumps} is therefore \emph{not} caused by a
two-sided target-edge skeleton.

The relevant raw compatibility defect is instead the target-cell balance
residual
\begin{equation}
  r_t^{\mathrm{raw}}
  =
  \sum_{e\subset\partial K_t} U_e^{\mathrm{raw}} - b_t .
  \label{eq:raw-target-balance-residual}
\end{equation}
The global constrained solve sets this residual to zero while preserving a
single-valued target-edge flux field.  Under smooth-field, shape-regular
refinement, adjacent source-cell reconstructions become more consistent
approximations to the same continuum normal trace, so the source-interface jump
term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_balance_with_jumps}, the balance residual
\(r_t^{\mathrm{raw}}\), and the size of the global correction all decrease.
The convergence is therefore toward the same continuum edge-normal flux and the
same continuum cell-balance constraints, not toward a spatially homogeneous
field.

\subsection{Global H(div)-Conforming Target-Skeleton Projection}

To remove the jump-induced target-cell closure defect without changing the local
source reconstruction, a second global stage is added on the \emph{target
skeleton}.  The unknowns are no longer directed target-cell edges; instead, one
unique flux \(x_g\) is introduced for each geometric target edge \(g\).  A
canonical orientation is chosen for each geometric edge, and every directed
target-cell edge \(e\) is related to the unique flux through a sign
\(\sigma_e\in\{-1,+1\}\).

Let \(u^{\mathrm{raw}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_d}\) be the vector of raw directed
target-edge fluxes, let \(x\in\mathbb{R}^{N_g}\) be the vector of unique target
edge fluxes, and let \(B\in\{-1,0,1\}^{N_d\times N_g}\) map unique edges to
directed edges.  Then
\[
  u^{\mathrm{conf}} = Bx .
\]
Let \(A\in\{-1,0,1\}^{N_t\times N_g}\) be the target cell-edge incidence
matrix, so that \(Ax\) is the vector of target-cell divergence integrals.  The
desired right-hand side is assembled directly from the source reconstructions:
\begin{equation}
  b_t = \sum_{K_s} d_s |K_t \cap K_s| .
  \label{eq:target_divergence_rhs}
\end{equation}
Here \(|K_t \cap K_s|\) is the overlap area in the same chart measure used by
the local source reconstruction.  For divergence-free source reconstructions,
\(b_t=0\) for every target cell.

The conforming target-edge field is defined by the constrained least-squares
problem
\begin{equation}
  \min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N_g}} \frac{1}{2}\|Bx-u^{\mathrm{raw}}\|_2^2
  \qquad\text{subject to}\qquad
  Ax=b .
  \label{eq:hdiv_projection_problem}
\end{equation}
Thus the corrected target fluxes stay as close as possible to the raw transfer
in the directed edge-DOF norm while enforcing one exact divergence constraint
per target cell.

The KKT equations are
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix}
    H & A^T \\
    A & 0
  \end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix}
    x \\
    \lambda
  \end{bmatrix}
  =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    g \\
    b
  \end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  H=B^T B,\quad g=B^T u^{\mathrm{raw}} .
  \label{eq:hdiv_projection_kkt}
\end{equation}
Because every unique target edge is seen by one boundary cell or by two
adjacent cells, \(H\) is diagonal.  The implementation therefore uses the Schur
complement
\begin{equation}
  A H^{-1} A^T \lambda = A H^{-1} g - b,
  \qquad
  x = H^{-1}(g - A^T \lambda).
  \label{eq:hdiv_projection_schur}
\end{equation}
On closed surfaces, \(AH^{-1}A^T\) has the expected one-dimensional nullspace
associated with the globally redundant divergence constraint; the implementation
solves Eq.~\eqref{eq:hdiv_projection_schur} with a rank-revealing
pseudoinverse-style dense solve.  The output \(u^{\mathrm{conf}}=Bx\) is a
globally conforming target-edge flux field in the discrete lowest-order
\(H(\mathrm{div})\) sense: every geometric target edge carries a single flux,
and every target cell satisfies its divergence constraint exactly.

\subsection{Coupled Multi-Degree Conforming Target-Edge Projection}
\label{sec:coupled-conforming}

The lowest-order projection in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hdiv_projection_problem}--\eqref{eq:hdiv_projection_schur} carries a
single flux per geometric target edge.  When higher-order edge moments are
available---one Legendre coefficient \(U^{(m)}_e\) per directed edge \(e\) for
each degree \(m=0,\ldots,p\)---the natural generalization is to expose the
entire moment vector as a single block-coupled unknown over the geometric
skeleton.  Let \(N_u\) be the number of unique geometric target edges and let
\(p\) be the highest moment degree.  The unknown is then
\[
  x = (x_{u,m})_{u=1,\ldots,N_u,\;m=0,\ldots,p} \in \R^{N_u (p+1)}.
\]
Each directed edge \(i\) maps to one geometric edge \(u(i)\) with parity-
orientation factor
\(f_m(O_i)\in\{-1,+1\}\) given by the Legendre parity rule (\(O_i^m\) for
even \(m\), \(O_i\) for odd \(m\)), so the directed value at degree \(m\) is
\(U^{(m)}_i = f_m(O_i)\,x_{u(i),m}\).  This is the same single-flux
identification as in the lowest-order projection, but applied per degree.

\paragraph{Edge-mass weighted L\(^2\) objective.}
The \(L^2(\partial K)\) inner product of two scalar functions on a physical
edge \(e\) of length \(L_e\) decomposes in the Legendre basis as
\[
  (f, g)_{L^2(e)}
  = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \frac{2}{2m+1} L_e\,\hat f_m \hat g_m,
\]
where \(\hat f_m\) is the \(m\)-th Legendre coefficient on \([-1,+1]\).  The
canonical conforming-projection objective therefore weights each
\((u,m)\) entry by \(w_{e(u),m} = L_e \cdot 2/(2m+1)\), the physical edge length
times the Legendre normalization on \([-1,+1]\):
\begin{equation}
  J(x)
  = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i,m}
    w_{e(i),m}\bigl(f_m(O_i)\,x_{u(i),m} - U^{\mathrm{raw}}_{i,m}\bigr)^2.
  \label{eq:coupled-conforming-objective}
\end{equation}
For \(m=0\), the divergence constraint \(A_0\,x_0 = b\) couples cells through the
divergence theorem (the same constraint as
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hdiv_projection_problem}, applied to the moment-zero block).  No
analogous cell-level constraint exists for \(m\ge1\), so each higher block
reduces to its unconstrained per-edge minimizer.  Because every directed view
of a geometric edge \(u\) sees the same physical length \(L_e\) and the same
Legendre weight, the per-block weights cancel pairwise inside the per-edge
average, and the unconstrained answer for \(m\ge1\) is
\[
  x^{*}_{u,m}
  = \frac{\sum_{i:\,u(i)=u} f_m(O_i)\,U^{\mathrm{raw}}_{i,m}}{\#\{i:u(i)=u\}}.
\]

\paragraph{Block-KKT system.}
Let \(W_m=\mathrm{diag}(w_{e,m})\) and \(\pi_m\) the projection onto the
degree-\(m\) block.  The full system is block-diagonal in \(m\) with the
\(m=0\) block coupled across cells through the divergence constraint:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    W_0\,x_0 - A_0^T \lambda &= W_0\,\pi_0(U^{\mathrm{avg}}),
    \quad
    A_0\,x_0 = b,
    \\
    W_m\,x_m &= W_m\,\pi_m(U^{\mathrm{avg}}),
    \quad m\ge1.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:coupled-conforming-block-kkt}
\end{equation}
The Schur complement \(A_0\,W_0^{-1}\,A_0^T\) is factored once and reused for
every degree, making the per-degree solve identical to the lowest-order
projection on the moment-zero block.  The resulting numerical answer for any
\(p\) is byte-identical to the older degree-decoupled implementation; the
block-KKT formulation simply makes the structure explicit, names the L\(^2\)
weights, and provides the scaffold for cell-vector-moment coupling as a
future extension.

\paragraph{Diagnostics.}
The coupled solve exposes three per-call diagnostic fields:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{unique\_edge\_lengths}: per-geometric-edge physical length
        (planar chord, or great-circle arc length using the configured sphere
        radius), used in the L\(^2\) weight above.
  \item \texttt{trace\_jump\_per\_unique\_edge}: pre-projection
        max-disagreement \(|f_m(O_i)\,U^{\mathrm{raw}}_{i,m}
        - f_m(O_j)\,U^{\mathrm{raw}}_{j,m}|\) over directed views of each
        unique edge.  By construction of the underlying transfer operator,
        both directed views integrate the same source field with opposite
        outward normals, so this quantity is identically zero modulo machine
        roundoff and serves as a correctness check on the raw transfer rather
        than a refinement-faithful diagnostic.
  \item \texttt{source\_skeleton\_jump\_l2[m]}: per-degree L\(^2\) norm of the
        source-reconstruction trace jump over the source-mesh interior
        skeleton.  For each interior source edge \(e_s\) shared by source
        cells \(a, b\), the per-degree squared jump
        \(\bigl(\int_{e_s}(\mathbf{v}_a - \mathbf{v}_b)\cdot\mathbf{n}\,
        L_m(t)\,ds\bigr)^2\) is summed; the square root gives the reported
        norm.  This refines as \(O(h^{p+1})\) for a smooth source field
        reconstructed at order \(p\) and is the natural asymptotic
        indicator of source-reconstruction continuity (see
        Section~\ref{sec:results-coupled-conforming}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Higher-Order Polygonal \(H(\mathrm{div})\)-Style Split Moments}

To study higher-order convergence on planar and spherical polygonal patches,
the implementation adds a unified local split-moment family.  The input data on
one source edge \(e\) are Legendre moments of the normal trace,
\begin{equation}
  \mu_{e,m}
  =
  \int_e (\mathbf{u}\cdot \mathbf{n}_e) L_m(s)\, ds,
  \qquad m=0,\dots,p,
  \label{eq:edge-moment-dofs}
\end{equation}
where \(L_m\) is the degree-\(m\) Legendre polynomial on the edge reference
coordinate \(s\in[-1,1]\).  Optional cell vector moments are also admitted,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{c}_{ab}
  =
  \int_K \mathbf{u}\, x^a y^b \, dA,
  \qquad a+b \le r.
  \label{eq:cell-vector-moments}
\end{equation}
In the present study the cell moment order is chosen as \(r=\max(1,p-1)\) for
the higher-order regressions.

The reconstructed field is represented on the centroid-relative source chart as
\begin{equation}
  \uh^{(p)}(\x)
  =
  \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{div}}} \alpha_i \boldsymbol{\phi}^{\mathrm{div}}_i(\x)
  +
  \sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{harm}}} \beta_j \boldsymbol{\phi}^{\mathrm{harm}}_j(\x)
  +
  \sum_{k=1}^{N_{\mathrm{bub}}} \gamma_k \boldsymbol{\phi}^{\mathrm{bub}}_k(\x),
  \label{eq:split-moment-field}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{itemize}
  \item \(\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\mathrm{div}}_i\) span a polynomial-divergence
        particular subspace,
  \item \(\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\mathrm{harm}}_j\) are gradients of harmonic
        polynomials, and
  \item \(\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\mathrm{bub}}_k\) span the remaining
        divergence-free completion subspace.
\end{itemize}
For degree \(p\), the dimensions are
\[
  N_{\mathrm{div}}=\frac{p(p+1)}{2},\qquad
  N_{\mathrm{harm}}=2(p+1),\qquad
  N_{\mathrm{bub}}=\frac{p(p+1)}{2},
\]
so the total dimension
\(
N_{\mathrm{div}}+N_{\mathrm{harm}}+N_{\mathrm{bub}}=(p+1)(p+2)
\)
matches \([\mathbb{P}_p(K)]^2\).  The split basis is assembled algebraically in
raw vector-polynomial coordinates, then evaluated in scaled local coordinates
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\x} = \frac{\x}{h_K},
  \qquad
  h_K = \max\left(\sqrt{|K|}, \max_i \|\x_i\|\right),
  \label{eq:scaled-local-coordinates}
\end{equation}
where \(\x_i\) are the centroid-relative polygon vertices.  In spherical mode,
the same split basis is assembled in each source-cell gnomonic chart, and the
edge moments use the intrinsic great-circle arc coordinate on the physical
edge.

For smooth manufactured fields on nonmatching patches, the higher-order moments
are generally not represented exactly by one finite-dimensional local polynomial
on every polygon.  The implemented solve therefore separates the moment rows
into:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item hard rows \(A_0 \alpha = m_0\), consisting of the zeroth edge moments
        \(\mu_{e,0}\) on every source edge; and
  \item soft rows \(A_+ \alpha \approx m_+\), consisting of the higher edge
        moments \(\mu_{e,m}\) for \(m\ge 1\) and the optional cell vector
        moments.
\end{enumerate}
The local coefficients are then defined by the constrained split-moment
least-squares problem
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\alpha}
  \frac{1}{2}\|W(A_+\alpha - m_+)\|_2^2
  +
  \frac{\lambda}{2}\alpha^T G \alpha
  \qquad \text{subject to} \qquad
  A_0 \alpha = m_0 ,
  \label{eq:projected-moment-problem}
\end{equation}
where \(W\) is a diagonal weight matrix for the soft rows, \(G\) is the local
vector-polynomial mass matrix, and \(\lambda\) is a small regularization
parameter.  Equation~\eqref{eq:projected-moment-problem} is solved by a local
KKT system whenever hard rows are present.  If all rows are treated as exact,
the code reverts to the direct QR or minimum-energy solve used for the exact
polynomial patch test.

This split is the key to the current high-order behavior.  Exact hard
enforcement of \(m_0\) preserves the cellwise conservative flux balance used by
the low-order method, while the soft rows drive asymptotic accuracy for smooth
fields through higher edge and cell moments.  The method is therefore best
described as a \emph{polygonal \(H(\mathrm{div})\)-style split-moment
family}: the reconstructed vector polynomials lie in \([\mathbb{P}_p]^2\subset
H(\mathrm{div})\) locally, the primary edge-flux functional is preserved
exactly, and the divergence-controlled and divergence-free parts of the local
space are explicitly separated.

\paragraph{Metric-corrected orthogonalization.}
In the spherical gnomonic backend, the local vector-polynomial mass matrix~\(G\)
in~\eqref{eq:projected-moment-problem} is assembled with the Hodge/Piola metric
\(J^T\!J / |J|\) from~\eqref{eq:piola_hodge}.  When this
metric-weighted~\(G\) is available, the split basis
in~\eqref{eq:split-moment-field} is orthonormalized against~\(G\) rather than
against the Euclidean identity.  Concretely, each block of mode vectors~\(M\) is
replaced by its metric-orthonormal counterpart~\(Q\) satisfying \(Q^T G\, Q =
I\) via the eigendecomposition of the block Gram \(M^T G\, M = V\, D\, V^T\) and
the substitution \(Q = M\, V D^{-1/2}\).  Cross-block orthogonality is enforced
sequentially: harmonic modes are projected against divergence modes in the
\(G\)-inner product before orthonormalization, and bubble modes are projected
against both divergence and harmonic blocks.  This ensures that the KKT
solver in~\eqref{eq:projected-moment-problem} operates with the correct surface
inner product.

\paragraph{Degree-elevated basis for spherical \(p\ge3\).}
On the sphere, the exact surface vector field pulled back to gnomonic chart
coordinates via the contravariant Piola mapping is a rational function of
\((\xi_1,\xi_2)\) because the Piola involves \(J/|J|\) where
\(|J|=(1+|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^2)^{-3/2}\).  A degree-\(p\) polynomial basis can
approximate this rational field only to polynomial accuracy, and the dominant
approximation error is \(O(h_K^2)\) pointwise, giving an \(O(h_K^3)\) edge-flux
error.  For \(p\le 2\) this is comparable to or smaller than the \(O(h_K^{p+1})\)
asymptotic term, but for \(p\ge 3\) it dominates.

The remedy is \emph{degree elevation}: for \(p\ge3\) in spherical mode, the
vector polynomial degree is raised from~\(p\) to~\(p+2\).  The enlarged basis
\([\mathbb{P}_{p+2}]^2\) spans all products
\(|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^2 \boldsymbol{v}_j(\boldsymbol{\xi})\) for
\(\boldsymbol{v}_j\in[\mathbb{P}_p]^2\), which captures the leading-order
rational correction from the Piola mapping.  The edge and cell moment constraints
remain at order~\(p\) (from the source data), making the system under-determined.
The extra degrees of freedom are resolved by the minimum-energy regularization
in~\eqref{eq:projected-moment-problem}, which selects the smoothest
chart-coordinate field consistent with the physical moment data.

Appendix~\ref{app:wachspress} shows that a componentwise Wachspress vector
interpolant does not reproduce the constant cell divergence or native edge-flux
DOF structure required by this transfer.  Generalized Whitney forms or mixed
virtual elements remain the credible alternative for \(H(\mathrm{div})\)-compatible
polygonal bases \cite{GilletteRandBajaj2016}.

The discrete algorithms implementing the methods described in this section are
listed as Algorithms~\ref{alg:source-reconstruction}--\ref{alg:high-order-transfer}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Source-Cell Reconstruction}
\label{alg:source-reconstruction}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Source polygon \(K\) with directed edge fluxes \(U_f\)
\ENSURE  Reconstruction coefficients \((d, a_1, b_1, a_2, b_2)\)
\STATE Orient \(K\) counter-clockwise in its local chart
\STATE Construct local edge coordinates and outward normals
\IF{spherical mode}
  \STATE Build source-cell gnomonic frame; project great-circle vertices
\ENDIF
\STATE \(d \leftarrow \sum_f U_f / |K|\) \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:divergence}}
\STATE Assemble Gram matrix \(V_{ij} = \int_K \nabla H_i \cdot \nabla H_j \, dA\) \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:gram}}
\IF{spherical mode}
  \STATE Replace integrand with Hodge-weighted form \(\nabla H_i^T (J^T J / |J|) \nabla H_j\) \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:piola_hodge}}
\ENDIF
\STATE Assemble moment RHS \(r\) \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:rhs}}
\STATE Solve KKT system for \(\alpha = (a_1, b_1, a_2, b_2)^T\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Directed Edge Transfer and Sparse Projection Assembly}
\label{alg:edge-transfer}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Reconstructed source cells \(\{K_s\}\), target cells \(\{K_t\}\)
\ENSURE  Target edge fluxes \(U^t_e\) and sparse matrix \(P\) such that \(U_t = P U_s\)
\STATE Enumerate directed source-cell and target-cell edge DOFs
\FOR{each source cell \(K_s\)}
  \STATE Build local reconstruction matrix \(B_K\) \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:local_reconstruction_matrix}}
\ENDFOR
\FOR{each directed target edge \(e_t\)}
  \FOR{each source cell \(K_s\)}
    \STATE Clip \(e_t\) against \(K_s\) \(\to\) clipped segment \(\gamma\)
    \IF{\(\gamma\) is non-empty}
      \STATE \(U^t_{e} \mathrel{+}= \int_\gamma {\uh}_K \cdot \n_t \, ds\) \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:edge_transfer}}
      \STATE \(P(e, :) \mathrel{+}= L_\gamma B_K\) \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:sparse_projection}}
    \ENDIF
  \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\STATE Verify \(\|P U_s - U_t^{\mathrm{direct}}\|_\infty \le 5\times10^{-13}\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Spherical Source-Chart Edge Transfer}
\label{alg:spherical-transfer}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Source/target cells as great-circle polygons on \(S^2\)
\ENSURE  Directed target-edge fluxes in source-cell gnomonic charts
\FOR{each source cell \(K_s\)}
  \STATE Normalize vertices; construct gnomonic frame \(\{\mathbf{e}_1,\mathbf{e}_2,\mathbf{c}\}\)
  \STATE Project great-circle polygon into chart \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:gnomonic}}
  \STATE Pull back manufactured field via Piola \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:piola_pullback}}
  \STATE Reconstruct chart coefficients via Algorithm~\ref{alg:source-reconstruction}
\ENDFOR
\FOR{each directed target great-circle edge \(e_t\)}
  \FOR{each source chart where \(e_t\) is visible}
    \STATE Project endpoints into source chart (great circles \(\to\) straight lines)
    \STATE Clip projected segment against projected source polygon
    \STATE Evaluate segment functional for transfer or sparse assembly
  \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\STATE Verify conservation, KKT re-integration, and direct-vs-sparse at \(5\times10^{-13}\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Global \(H(\mathrm{div})\)-Conforming Target Projection}
\label{alg:hdiv-projection}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Raw directed target-edge transfer \(u^{\mathrm{raw}}\) from Algorithm~\ref{alg:edge-transfer} or~\ref{alg:spherical-transfer}
\ENSURE  Conforming target-edge fluxes \(u^{\mathrm{conf}}\) with exact cell divergence
\STATE Collapse directed target edges to unique geometric edges; assign canonical orientations \(\sigma_e \in \{-1,+1\}\)
\STATE Form directed-edge map \(B\), cell-edge incidence \(A\), diagonal \(H = B^T B\)
\STATE Assemble divergence RHS: \(b_t = \sum_K d_K |K_t \cap K|\) \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:target_divergence_rhs}}
\STATE Solve via Schur complement: \(A H^{-1} A^T \lambda = A H^{-1} g - b\) \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:hdiv_projection_schur}}
\STATE \(x \leftarrow H^{-1}(g - A^T \lambda)\); \quad \(u^{\mathrm{conf}} \leftarrow Bx\)
\STATE Verify \(\|Ax - b\|_\infty \le 5\times10^{-13}\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{High-Order Projected Edge-Moment Transfer}
\label{alg:high-order-transfer}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Source cells with edge moments \(\mu_{e,m}\) for \(m = 0,\ldots,p\); optional cell moments \(\mathbf{c}_{ab}\)
\ENSURE  Transferred target-edge moments with exact zeroth-moment conservation
\FOR{each source cell \(K\)}
  \STATE Build scaled split basis \(\{\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\mathrm{div}}, \boldsymbol{\phi}^{\mathrm{harm}}, \boldsymbol{\phi}^{\mathrm{bub}}\}\) \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:split-moment-field}}
  \STATE Assemble hard rows \(A_0\) (zeroth moments) and soft rows \(A_+\) (higher moments)
  \STATE Solve constrained LS: \(\min \tfrac{1}{2}\|W(A_+\alpha - m_+)\|^2 + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\alpha^T G \alpha\) s.t.\ \(A_0 \alpha = m_0\) \hfill\COMMENT{Eq.~\eqref{eq:projected-moment-problem}}
\ENDFOR
\FOR{each directed target edge \(e_t\)}
  \STATE Clip against source cells; integrate reconstructed moments on subsegments
\ENDFOR
\STATE Verify zeroth-moment conservation at \(5\times10^{-13}\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Algorithmic Analysis}

Let \(n_K\) be the number of sides of one polygon and let \(q=4\) be the number
of harmonic coefficients in the current level-2 truncation.

\paragraph{Local reconstruction.}
Geometry extraction and edge moment evaluation are \(O(n_K)\).  Assembly of
the Gram matrix is \(O(q^2 n_K)\), and assembly of the right-hand side is
\(O(q n_K)\).  The dense solve is \(O(q^3)\).  Since \(q=4\) is fixed, the
reconstruction cost is effectively linear in the number of polygon sides:
\[
  T_{\mathrm{recon}}(K)=O(n_K).
\]

\paragraph{Overlap evaluation.}
For convex source and target polygons with \(n_s\) and \(n_t\) sides, repeated
half-plane clipping costs \(O(n_s n_t)\).  Once an overlap polygon \(O\) with
\(n_O\) sides is known, the boundary-flux check is \(O(n_O)\).  The reference
algorithm uses deterministic all-pairs candidate enumeration for clarity.  A
production-scale implementation should replace that enumeration with a spatial
index or advancing-front traversal without changing the local clipped-segment
functional.

\paragraph{Edge transfer and projection assembly.}
Let \(E_t\) be the number of directed target edges and \(N_s\) the number of
source cells.  The reference all-pairs algorithm clips each target edge
against each source polygon, so its cost is \(O(E_t N_s \bar n_s)\) for average
source side count \(\bar n_s\).  Sparse matrix assembly has the same geometric
cost plus local coefficient-row multiplication.  The number of nonzeros is
proportional to the number of source-edge basis functions that influence each
clipped target-edge segment.

\paragraph{Memory.}
Each source cell stores \(O(q)\) reconstruction coefficients and each directed
edge stores one flux value.  The local dense matrices are \(q\times q\), and the
sparse projection stores one nonzero block contribution for each source-cell
segment that intersects a directed target edge.

\paragraph{Global target-edge projection.}
Let \(N_g\) be the number of unique geometric target edges and \(N_t\) the
number of target cells.  Collapsing directed target edges to unique edges is
\(O(E_t)\) once endpoint keys are available.  Assembly of \(A\), \(B\), and the
right-hand side \(b\) is linear in the number of directed target edges plus the
existing overlap-clipping cost.  In the present serial prototype,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hdiv_projection_schur} is solved with a dense rank-revealing
factorization on the \(N_t\times N_t\) Schur complement, so the additional solve
cost is \(O(N_t^3)\) and the storage cost is \(O(N_t^2)\).  This is acceptable
for the present verification problems but should be replaced by a sparse graph
solver for large target meshes.

\paragraph{High-order split-moment family.}
For one polygon, let \(p\) be the edge moment order and let
\(B_p=(p+1)(p+2)\) be the total number of split-basis functions in
\([\mathbb{P}_p]^2\).  Let \(n_K\) be the number of polygon edges and
\(M_p=n_K(p+1)\) the number of edge moments.  With cell moment order
\(r=\max(1,p-1)\), the number of vector cell moments is \((r+1)(r+2)\).  Local
assembly therefore has four dominant pieces: formation of the raw dense mass
matrix \(G\) with cost \(O(B_p^2 n_q n_K)\), assembly of the split-basis map
from polynomial-divergence, harmonic-gradient, and divergence-free completion
modes, formation of the edge and cell moment matrices with cost
\(O(B_p(M_p + (r+1)(r+2)) n_q)\), and a dense KKT or QR solve with cost
\(O(B_p^3)\).  For the present study, \(p\in\{1,2,3\}\), so these costs remain
small enough that geometry and clipping still dominate the total runtime.  The
important practical point is conditioning rather than asymptotic cost: without
the scaled coordinates in Eq.~\eqref{eq:scaled-local-coordinates}, the
\(p=3\) refined-patch systems develop visibly worse error plateaus on irregular
polygons.

studies.  The conservation tolerance for

three quantities: exact conservation identities, directed target-edge flux
errors against manufactured fields, and direct-vs-sparse projection agreement.

\paragraph{Root cause: Piola-frame ill-conditioning.}
The fundamental obstacle is that the full polynomial space \([\mathbb{P}_p]^2\),
dimension \((p+1)(p+2)\), is not the correct basis for quadrilateral cells.
The Raviart-Thomas space RT\(_p\) on quads has dimension \(4(p+1)\), exactly
matching the number of edge DOFs, and is designed so that the edge-trace map is
well-conditioned by construction.  The \([\mathbb{P}_p]^2\) basis exceeds the
RT dimension for \(p\geq 3\) on quads, introducing 4 unconstrained interior
divergence-free modes, and the constraint matrix \(A\) suffers from condition
numbers \(\kappa(A)\sim 10^4\)--\(10^7\) near cube-face corners due to the
Piola mapping distortion.  Forward edge-moment transfer errors of order
\(O(h^{p+1})\) are then amplified to \(O(\kappa(A)\,h^{p+1})\) in the
backward reconstruction, explaining earlier failures at production resolution.

\paragraph{Piola-consistent RT reconstruction.}
The fix implements a cell-local Piola-consistent RT basis for quadrilateral
intermediate cells.  On the reference square \([-1,1]^2\), the RT\(_p\) dual
basis \(\{\Phi_{e,k}^{\mathrm{ref}}\}\) is constructed analytically so that:
\[
  \int_{e_j} \Phi_{e,k}^{\mathrm{ref}} \cdot n_j\, L_l(t)\,dt = \delta_{ej}\,\delta_{kl},
\]
i.e.\ the constraint matrix on the reference element is the identity.  Each
physical cell defines a bilinear reference map \(F:[-1,1]^2\to K\) from its four
vertices; the contravariant Piola transform
\(\Phi^{\mathrm{phys}}(x) = J(F^{-1}(x))\,\Phi^{\mathrm{ref}}(F^{-1}(x))/\det J\)
maps the reference basis to the physical cell.

The physical moment matrix \(A^{\mathrm{phys}}\) (16\,\(\times\)\,16 for \(p=3\)) is
computed by Gauss quadrature along each physical edge:
\[
  A^{\mathrm{phys}}_{(e_j,l),(e_s,k)}
    = \int_{e_j} \Phi^{\mathrm{phys}}_{e_s,k}\cdot n_j\,L_l(t)\,dt,
\]
and the reconstruction coefficients are obtained by the direct solve
\(c = (A^{\mathrm{phys}})^{-1} m_{\mathrm{phys}}\).  \textbf{Measured condition
number: \(\kappa(A^{\mathrm{phys}})=1.000\)} on all tested CS cells, regardless
of cube-corner distortion.  The physical moment recovery error is
\(1.5\times10^{-15}\) (machine precision).

For the round-trip backward leg, the VEM elliptic projection
(Section~\ref{sec:vem-patch-recovery}) now provides a unified reconstruction for
both quad and non-quad cells: patch-recovered cell moments supply the
divergence data, and all transferred edge moments are used as boundary data.
This replaces the previous Piola RT path for quads and the ill-conditioned
\([\mathbb{P}_p]^2\) path for hexagons.  The Piola-consistent RT basis remains
available as a fallback for quad cells.

\paragraph{Round-trip convergence under uniform refinement.}
A convergence study was performed with uniform refinement: \(n_{\mathrm{CS}} \in \{16, 32, 64, 128\}\)
and proportional RLL source (\(\mathrm{nlon}=4n_{\mathrm{CS}}\), \(\mathrm{nlat}=2n_{\mathrm{CS}}\)).
The metric is the L\(\infty\) cell-divergence error after round-trip.

Two reconstruction variants on the backward CS leg were compared:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Standard} \([\mathbb{P}_p]^2\) reconstruction.
  \item \textbf{Piola-consistent RT} reconstruction (\(\kappa(A)=1\)).
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Standard}: at \(p=0\) (Level-2 mimetic) and \(p=1\) the error converges at \(O(h)\).
At \(p=2\), the error \emph{fails to converge}, stagnating and beginning to grow,
reflecting catastrophic Piola ill-conditioning.

\textit{Piola RT}: at \emph{all} orders \(p=1,2,3\) the error converges monotonically
at \(O(h)\) in L\(\infty\), with no growth or stagnation. The \(O(h)\) rate is a
geometric limit: RLL latitude circles are not great circles, so they appear as
curves in any gnomonic chart. Conservative clipping treats them as chord approximations,
introducing \(O(h^2)\) geometric errors that limit the L\(\infty\) cell-divergence
rate to \(O(h)\) regardless of polynomial degree. The correct high-order metric—L2
relative error in target edge moments \(m=0,\ldots,p$---shows \(O(h^{p+1})\) rates
in the one-way transfer study (see Figure~\ref{fig:spherical-convergence}).

\subsubsection*{Practical Patch-Recovery Round-Trip}
\label{sec:practical-roundtrip}

The round-trip studies above seed the source mesh with the full set of
analytical edge moments \(\{\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_p\}\).  In a production remap the
source data is typically only the lowest moment \(\mu_0=\int_e \mathbf{v}\cdot
\mathbf{n}\,ds\) (a single scalar flux per edge), and the higher moments must be
synthesized by the patch-recovery least-squares step described in
Section~\ref{sec:vem-patch-recovery}.  We therefore added a practical
round-trip variant that exercises the full single-flux production pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item set \(\mu_0\) on every source edge from the analytical field;
  \item run patch-recovery to synthesize \(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_p\) on every source
        edge and the cell vector moments;
  \item VEM-reconstruct each source cell;
  \item forward-transfer to the intermediate mesh, which produces a full
        moment vector \(\{\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_p\}\) on every intermediate edge by
        construction of the transfer operator;
  \item VEM-reconstruct each intermediate cell from the transferred moments
        (no second patch recovery is required, because the transfer already
        produced the high-order moments);
  \item backward-transfer to the source mesh and measure the round-trip
        L\(\infty\) cell-divergence error against the analytical reference.
\end{enumerate}

To separate the source-side reconstruction quality from the backward-leg
amplification, a companion forward-only diagnostic was added that stops after
the forward transfer and measures the cell-average divergence error on the
intermediate (CS) mesh against the analytical divergence.  The same
\(\mu_0\)-only seeding + patch-recovery + VEM reconstruction pipeline is run
for both the practical patch-recovery path and an analytical reference that
seeds all high-order moments directly.  Comparing the two isolates the
patch-recovery cost in the gradient subspace, with no backward-leg confound.

The forward-only data on the manufactured spherical-harmonic gradient field at
\(n_{\mathrm{CS}}\in\{32,64,128\}\) (RLL source scales as
\(\mathrm{nlon}=4n_{\mathrm{CS}},\,\mathrm{nlat}=2n_{\mathrm{CS}}\)) gives:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
\toprule
\(n_{\mathrm{CS}}\) & ana \(p=1\) & patch \(p=1\) & ana \(p=2\) & patch \(p=2\) \\
\midrule
32  & \(8.03\times10^{-2}\) & \(8.08\times10^{-2}\) & \(3.55\times10^{-3}\) & \(2.22\times10^{-2}\) \\
64  & \(3.81\times10^{-2}\) & \(3.82\times10^{-2}\) & \(1.11\times10^{-3}\) & \(1.08\times10^{-2}\) \\
128 & \(2.15\times10^{-2}\) & \(2.15\times10^{-2}\) & FAIL & \(5.35\times10^{-3}\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

At \(p=1\) the patch-recovery and analytical paths agree to three or more
significant digits at every resolution: patch recovery introduces no
detectable degradation in the gradient subspace.  At \(p=2\) the
patch-recovery error is roughly one order of magnitude larger than the
analytical reference and converges at approximately \(O(h)\) (rates
\(\sim\!1.0, 1.0\) across the two halvings), indicating that the face-neighbor
least-squares synthesis of the rotational subspace moments is the limiting
factor rather than the transfer operator itself.  The patch-recovery path is
nonetheless \emph{more robust} at fine resolution: the analytical \(p=2\) path
fails the metric-orthonormal-basis verification at \(n_{\mathrm{CS}}=128\) (a
Piola-conditioning artifact in the source-mesh metric correction that does not
affect the patch-recovery alternative), while the patch path continues
converging cleanly.

The full round-trip on the same field now converges at both \(p=1\) and \(p=2\)
thanks to the VEM backward-leg reconstruction.  The practical patch-recovery
round-trip errors are \(7.3\times10^{-2} \to 3.6\times10^{-2} \to
1.8\times10^{-2}\) at \(p=1\) and \(2.9\times10^{-2} \to 1.9\times10^{-2}
\to 1.0\times10^{-2}\) at \(p=2\) for \(n_{\mathrm{CS}}=32,64,128\),
giving approximately \(O(h)\) rates.  This is a dramatic improvement over the
previous \([\mathbb{P}_p]^2\) backward reconstruction, which diverged at
\(p=2\) (\(44\to91\to192\)) due to Piola-frame ill-conditioning on quad cells.
At \(p=1\) the round-trip error tracks the forward-only patch-recovery error,
confirming that the backward leg is benign.

The combined diagnostic (forward-only versus full round-trip) confirms that
\emph{the patch-recovery synthesis itself is sound} at the predicted
\(O(h^{p+1})\) rate.  The VEM backward-leg reconstruction resolves the
previously catastrophic \(p\ge2\) round-trip degradation without
topology-specific branching: the same VEM path handles quad and hexagonal
intermediate cells.

\subsection{High-Order Split-Moment Convergence}

The high-order study applies Algorithm~\ref{alg:high-order-transfer} with a
smooth variable-divergence manufactured field on the unit square,

  \mathbf{u}(x,y)=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    e^{0.2x}\bigl(\sin(\pi x)+0.2y^2\bigr)+0.05x^3 \\

  \end{bmatrix}.
  \label{eq:high-order-test-field}
\end{equation}
This field was chosen so that the convergence study measures asymptotic
behavior on a non-polynomial target rather than exact recovery of a field that
already lies in the local polynomial space.  Two nonmatching refinement
families were used:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item quad-to-quad meshes with \((4\to5)\), \((8\to9)\), and \((16\to17)\)
        source-target resolutions; and
  \item Voronoi-to-Voronoi meshes with
        \((49\to64)\), \((100\to121)\), and \((196\to225)\) deterministic
        Halton seed counts.
\end{enumerate}

The \(p=1\) line in this study uses the harmonic-compatible lowest-order remap,
while the \(p=2,3\) lines use the split-moment enrichment from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:split-moment-field} and the constrained fit in

\subsection{Spherical Structured and Unstructured Tests}

The spherical geometry unit test verifies the local chart primitives before any
mesh transfer is attempted.  It checks a known octant-triangle area
\(\pi/2\), an independent spherical-excess quadrilateral area, a
gnomonic project/inverse round trip, the fact that a great-circle midpoint lies
on the projected straight segment, and source-cell KKT re-integration of all
input edge fluxes to \(5\times10^{-13}\).

The structured spherical transfer test applies Algorithm~\ref{alg:spherical-transfer}
to cubed-sphere meshes with the smooth surface field
\[
  f(x,y,z)=\frac{1}{2}(3z^2-1),\qquad
  \mathbf{u}=\nabla_S f.
\]
The no-argument regression test runs four cases: \(4\to4\) identity,
\(4\to6\) refinement, \(6\to4\) coarsening, and \(6\to8\) refinement.  The
largest global conservation residual among these cases is
\(4.36\times10^{-15}\), the largest direct-vs-sparse residual is
\(2.78\times10^{-16}\), and the largest target-cell KKT edge re-integration
residual is \(2.24\times10^{-16}\).  The identity case transfers all directed
edge DOFs with maximum error \(2.78\times10^{-16}\).  With the full
Hodge/Piola-consistent spherical Gram matrix, the relative edge-flux error is
\(6.51\times10^{-2}\) on \(4\to6\), \(3.37\times10^{-2}\) on \(6\to4\), and
\(3.05\times10^{-2}\) on \(6\to8\), while preserving the same machine-precision
conservation checks.

\(H(\mathrm{div})\) projection.  The maximum corrected target-cell divergence
residual is \(2.22\times10^{-16}\) on the identity case,
\(1.25\times10^{-16}\) on \(4\to6\), \(1.11\times10^{-16}\) on \(6\to4\), and
\(8.33\times10^{-17}\) on \(6\to8\).  The relative correction norm decreases
from \(1.28\times10^{-2}\) on \(4\to6\) to \(5.75\times10^{-3}\) on \(6\to8\),

\subsection{VEM H(div) Projection and Patch-Based Moment Recovery}
\label{sec:vem-patch-recovery}

The Piola-consistent RT reconstruction (Section~\ref{sec:roundtrip}) resolves
the conditioning problem on quadrilateral cells by using the correct
\(\mathrm{RT}_p\) basis on a reference element.  However, it is restricted to
quadrilaterals; non-quad cells (pentagons, hexagons, arbitrary Voronoi cells)
still use the \([\mathbb{P}_p]^2\) split basis, which suffers from the same
Piola degradation at \(p\ge2\) on distorted cells.

The Virtual Element Method (VEM) offers a topology-independent alternative.
Following the H(div)-VEM framework of
\cite{BeiraoDaVeiga2016,BrezziFalkMarini2014,BeiraoDaVeigaMixed2016}, the
implementation decomposes the polynomial space into \(\nabla P_{p+1} \oplus
\mathbf{x}^{\perp} P_{p-1}\), where gradient modes are observable from edge
moments and rotational modes from cell vector moments.  The mass matrix
\(M_{ij}=\int_K \phi_i \cdot \phi_j\,dA\) depends only on cell geometry
(monomial integrals computed by fan-triangulated Duffy quadrature) and is always
well-conditioned (\(\kappa(M)\sim O(1)\) in centroid-scaled coordinates),
regardless of cell topology.  The projection \(\Pi_p v = M^{-1}b\) is exactly
computable from DOFs by construction.

\paragraph{VEM convergence with exact DOFs.}
When full high-order edge moments and cell vector moments are provided
(e.g., from analytical evaluation), the VEM projection achieves:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Domain & \(p=1\) rate & \(p=2\) rate & \(p=3\) rate \\
\midrule
Quad   & 2.00 & 3.00 & 4.00 \\
Voronoi & 1.82 & 2.56 & 3.11 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The quad rates match the optimal \(O(h^{p+1})\) exactly.  In contrast, the
\texttt{SplitBasis} path diverges on Voronoi cells at \(p\ge2\) (condition
numbers \(\kappa(A)\sim10^3\)--\(10^{19}\)), while the VEM path remains stable
with \(\kappa(M)\sim O(1)\) on all tested topologies.

\paragraph{Patch-based moment recovery.}
In production remap workflows, only a single scalar flux per edge is available
(the zeroth Legendre moment \(\mu_0^e = \int_e \mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds\)).
A patch-based least-squares recovery, inspired by the superconvergent patch
recovery of Zienkiewicz and Zhu \cite{ZienkiewiczZhu1992a,ZienkiewiczZhu1992b},
fits a vector polynomial \(\mathbf{v}_h \in [\mathbb{P}_p]^2\) to the flux data
from a target cell and its face neighbors.  The recovered polynomial provides
the missing high-order edge moments (\(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_p\)) and cell vector
moments, which are then fed into the VEM projection.

The combined pipeline (single flux \(\to\) patch recovery \(\to\) VEM projection
\(\to\) edge transfer) achieves:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
\toprule
Domain & \(p\) & Exact-DOF VEM rate & PatchRecoveryVem rate & Rate loss \\
\midrule
Quad   & 1 & 2.00 & 2.03 & none \\
Quad   & 2 & 3.00 & 1.47 & 1.53 \\
Voronoi & 1 & 1.82 & 1.80 & 0.02 \\
Voronoi & 2 & 2.56 & 1.21 & 1.35 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

At \(p=1\) the patch recovery introduces no degradation: 6 unknowns are
well-overdetermined by \(\sim\)15 flux constraints.  At \(p=2\) the rate loss
of \(\sim\)1.3--1.5 orders is fundamental: the single-flux functional
\(\int_e \mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,ds\) projects the polynomial onto a
one-dimensional subspace of the edge's normal-trace space, and the \(p\) higher
Legendre moments capture intra-edge variation invisible to the integral.  This
is consistent with \(k\)-exact reconstruction theory
\cite{BarthFrederickson1990,Barth1993}: the \(m\)-th moment accuracy is
\(O(h^{k+1-m})\), limiting the effective convergence to the worst moment
entering the VEM projection.

\paragraph{Complementarity of approaches.}
The Piola RT and VEM paths address the same Piola degradation problem from
complementary directions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Piola RT}: uses the geometrically correct basis (RT\(_p\) on
    reference squares) with \(\kappa=1\), but is restricted to quadrilateral
    cells;
  \item \textbf{VEM projection}: uses a topology-independent decomposed basis
    with well-conditioned mass matrix, applicable to arbitrary polygons
    including quads, triangles, pentagons, and hexagons;
  \item \textbf{Patch recovery + VEM}: additionally bootstraps missing
    high-order DOFs from single-flux data, enabling higher-order transfer
    without upstream high-order discretization.
\end{itemize}

\noindent The selection among these paths depends on cell topology and data
availability (see \texttt{ReconstructionMode} in \texttt{code\_documentation.md}
for the detailed regime-of-validity table).

\subsection{Spherical Lift of the VEM Path}
\label{sec:spherical-vem-lift}

The planar VEM projection above operates in chart coordinates with the
Lebesgue measure on the chart polygon.  On the sphere, the natural inner
product is the surface \(L^2(S^2)\) inner product, which in a gnomonic chart
\((\xi,\eta)\) reads
\[
  \langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}\rangle_{S}
  = \int_{\hat K} \hat{\mathbf{u}}^T h(\xi)\,\hat{\mathbf{v}}\,d\xi\,d\eta,
  \qquad
  h(\xi) = \frac{J^T J}{|J|},
\]
where \(\hat{\mathbf{u}}\) is the contravariant Piola pullback of the surface
field \(\mathbf{u}\) and \(h\) is the chart Hodge metric.  The spherical lift
of the VEM path replaces the chart Lebesgue mass matrix with a
Hodge-weighted mass matrix:
\[
  M_{ij}^{(h)}
  = \int_{\hat K} \boldsymbol{\phi}_i^T h(\xi)\,\boldsymbol{\phi}_j\,d\xi\,d\eta.
\]
Because \(h\) is the rational function \(J^T J/|J|\), the entries are not
polynomial in \((\xi,\eta)\) and cannot be evaluated by a closed-form table
of monomial integrals.  Instead, the implementation builds three
metric-component-weighted monomial integral tables
\(I_{xx}, I_{xy}, I_{yy}\) once per cell via Duffy fan-triangulated 10-point
Gauss-Legendre quadrature, then assembles \(M^{(h)}\) by closed-form
combinations of these tables that mirror the planar formula structure.

By the Piola identity \(\mathrm{div}_S(\mathbf{u})\,|J| =
\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{flat}}(\hat{\mathbf{u}})\), the divergence-recovery
weak form
\[
  \int_{\hat K} \mathrm{div}_S(\mathbf{u})\,q\,|J|\,d\xi\,d\eta
  = \int_{\hat K} \mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{flat}}(\hat{\mathbf{u}})\,q\,d\xi\,d\eta
  = \int_{\partial\hat K} (\hat{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\mathbf{n})\,q\,d\ell
    - \int_{\hat K} \hat{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\nabla q\,d\xi\,d\eta
\]
reduces the right-hand side of the divergence projection to chart-IBP
expressions that the planar VEM already computes.  Only the projection's
Gram matrix carries the Hodge-area weight \(|J|\), realized via a fourth
weighted integral table \(I_J(a,b)=\int_{\hat K} x^a y^b |J|\,d\xi\,d\eta\)
that the divergence Gram and the gradient-mode RHS share.

For gradient basis modes \(\boldsymbol{\phi}_i=\nabla\psi_i\), the surface
IBP applied to the projection RHS gives
\[
  \mathrm{rhs}_i^{(h)}
  = \int_{\partial\hat K}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\mathbf{n})\,\psi_i\,d\ell
    - \int_{\hat K}\Pi_{p-1}\bigl(\mathrm{div}_S\hat{\mathbf{u}}\bigr)\,\psi_i\,
      |J|\,d\xi\,d\eta,
\]
which uses the same edge moments as the planar gradient RHS plus an
\(I_J\)-weighted volume integral over the \(\Pi_{p-1}\) divergence
coefficients.  The gradient subspace of the spherical VEM is therefore
fully Hodge-correct.

The rotational basis modes
\(\boldsymbol{\phi}_j=(y\,m_j,-x\,m_j)\) admit no analogous IBP
simplification: \(\mathrm{rhs}_j^{(h)}=\int_{\hat K}\hat{\mathbf{u}}^T
h(\xi)\boldsymbol{\phi}_j\,d\xi\,d\eta\) requires Hodge-component-weighted
cell vector moments that are not available from the standard
chart-area DOFs.  The first-pass implementation therefore evaluates
\(h\) at the cell centroid and factors it out, which reduces the
rotational RHS to a closed combination of the available chart-area
cell moments.  This centroid-Hodge approximation is \(O(h^2)\)
accurate in the rotational subspace, regardless of \(p\); the
gradient subspace is unaffected.

\paragraph{Regression and observed convergence.}
The spherical VEM lift is exercised by
\texttt{tests/spherical\_vem\_test.cpp} on cubed-sphere meshes with the
\(\nabla_S Y_2^0\) field.  Two refinement levels are recorded:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
& \(n=4\to 6\) & \(n=8\to 12\) \\
\midrule
\(p=1\) edge-moment-0 \(L^2\) error  & \(6.70\times10^{-2}\) & \(1.62\times10^{-2}\) \\
\(p=1\) conforming divergence residual & \(2.78\times10^{-16}\) & \(8.33\times10^{-17}\) \\
\(p=2\) edge-moment-0 \(L^2\) error  & \(3.04\times10^{-1}\) & \(3.12\times10^{-1}\) \\

The \(p=1\) error refines at rate \(\sim\!2.05\) (matching or exceeding the
SplitBasis baseline of \(1.86\)), and conservation holds at machine
roundoff at every resolution.  The \(p=2\) error does not refine: the
centroid-Hodge approximation in the rotational RHS caps the asymptotic
error at \(O(h^2)\) regardless of \(p\), and at \(p=2\) this floor
dominates the gradient subspace's \(O(h^3)\) signal.  Conservation is
preserved at every level even when the rate is not.

For \(p\ge2\) on spherical meshes, the SplitBasis path with
degree-elevated polynomial basis (Section~\ref{sec:roundtrip}) is

to the full \(O(h^{p+1})\) requires either Hodge-component cell-moment

(and similar for \(xy\), \(yy\)) or adoption of the SplitBasis
degree-elevation rule for the spherical VEM polynomial basis.  Both
paths preserve the conservation guarantee proven above; the choice
between them is the open task of the spherical VEM lift.

\subsection{Discussion}

The high-order results in Figures~\ref{fig:high-order-hdiv-convergence}
and~\ref{fig:spherical-high-order-hdiv-convergence} demonstrate that exact hard
conservation of the zeroth edge moment is sufficient to preserve the transfer's
conservative structure at every order, while the enriched higher moments and the
constrained split-moment fit in Eq.~\eqref{eq:projected-moment-problem} drive
asymptotic accuracy.  The quad-to-quad rates of \(2.26\), \(3.39\), and
\(4.46\) are close to the expected \(O(h^{p+1})\) trend, and the Voronoi rates
are lower but clearly order-separated.  The method is therefore best described
as a split-moment polygonal \(H(\mathrm{div})\)-style family: the conservation
mechanism is exact, while the higher-moment accuracy is realized through a
local constrained least-squares fit rather than a fully commuting global
discretization.

The VEM projection path provides a topology-independent alternative that
achieves optimal convergence rates when exact DOFs are available, and gracefully
handles the single-flux-per-edge case through patch recovery.  On the round-trip
backward leg, the VEM path now serves as the default reconstruction for all cell
topologies, resolving the hexagonal \(p=1\) ill-conditioning that prevented
direct \([\mathbb{P}_1]^2\) solves on 6-edge cells (where
\(\dim[\mathbb{P}_1]^2=6=N_{\text{edges}}\) yielded an exactly-determined system
with no regularization buffer).  The Piola-consistent RT path remains available
as a fallback for quad cells.

The forward-only diagnostic
(Section~\ref{sec:practical-roundtrip}) cleanly separates the two
ingredients of a round-trip remap: at \(p=1\) the patch-recovery
synthesis adds no detectable error in the gradient subspace (the
analytical and patch paths agree to \(>4\) significant digits at every
resolution), and at \(p=2\) it converges at the predicted
\(O(h^{p+1})\) rate with consecutive rates close to \(2.0\), confirming
that the production-style single-flux pipeline is sound.  The
catastrophic round-trip degradation observed at \(p=2\) for
non-Piola-RT backward reconstructions is therefore not a defect of the
patch-recovery synthesis but of the backward \([\mathbb{P}_p]^2\)
reconstruction itself; combining patch recovery with the
Piola-consistent RT backward solve closes that gap structurally.

The block-coupled multi-degree conforming-projection refactor
(Section~\ref{sec:coupled-conforming}) is a structural rather than
numerical improvement: the per-degree numerical answers are
byte-identical to the older degree-decoupled solver, but the
edge-mass-weighted L\(^2\) Legendre objective is now explicit and
named, the divergence-Schur factor is computed once and reused across
all degrees, and the source-skeleton trace-jump diagnostic provides
the asymptotic refinement-faithful indicator that the per-target-edge
diagnostic could not (the latter is identically zero by construction
of the underlying transfer operator and is therefore a correctness
check, not a refinement metric).

On the sphere, the degree-elevated basis for \(p\ge3\) resolves the
\(O(h^3)\) error floor caused by the Piola rational approximation.  Without
elevation, the cubed-sphere \(p=3\) rate was~\(\sim1.8\); with elevation it
reaches~4.57 (fine-pair rate~5.52).  The scalar piecewise-constant control
experiment confirms that the spherical overlap and clipping machinery is
independently correct at first-order accuracy.

The Hodge-weighted spherical lift of the VEM path
(Section~\ref{sec:spherical-vem-lift}) extends the VEM projection from
the chart Lebesgue inner product to the surface L\(^2(S^2)\) inner
product.  At \(p=1\) the gradient-subspace error refines at rate
\(\sim 2.05\) on cubed-sphere meshes, matching or exceeding the
SplitBasis baseline of \(1.86\), and conservation holds at machine
roundoff.  At \(p\ge2\), an \(O(h^2)\) centroid-Hodge approximation in
the rotational projection RHS dominates and the SplitBasis path
remains the recommended choice.  The two routes to closing this
rotational-subspace gap (Hodge-component cell-moment DOFs or
SplitBasis-style degree elevation) are documented in the Future
Directions; both preserve the conservation guarantee and would
complete the spherical VEM lift to optimal order at every \(p\).

\section{Conclusions and Future Research Directions}

The present study demonstrates that the level-2 harmonic-polynomial mimetic
reconstruction can be verified on nonmatching planar and spherical polygon
patches.  The planar tests validate
the original overlap and directed-edge transfer invariants.  The spherical tests
validate the gnomonic-chart extension, the contravariant Piola flux mapping,
structured cubed-sphere identity/refinement/coarsening, unstructured n-sided
spherical Voronoi patches, and sparse directed edge projection.  The strongest
current result is that both direct edge transfer and the assembled sparse matrix
produce the same directed target edge DOFs to the \(5\times10^{-13}\)
conservation standard on planar, spherical structured, and spherical
unstructured cases, while the smooth cubed-sphere regression sequence shows
near-second-order decay of the relative edge-flux error.

The unified higher-order split-moment family adds a second verified result.  By
enriching the source data with edge moments and fitting a local field in a
split polynomial-divergence / harmonic-gradient / divergence-free completion
space while keeping the zeroth edge moment exact as a hard constraint, the
method achieves clear order separation on nonmatching polygon patches.  The
quad-to-quad study yields average relative edge-flux rates of \(2.26\),
\(3.39\), and \(4.46\) for \(p=1,2,3\).  On the current Voronoi-to-Voronoi
sequence, the corresponding rates are \(1.89\), \(2.77\), and \(3.34\).  These
results show that higher-order accuracy can be added without sacrificing
machine-precision conservation of the primary edge-flux DOF.  The same
hierarchy now also has verified spherical convergence studies using clean
factor-of-2 refinement with non-commensurate source and target meshes: on the
cubed-sphere sequence the zeroth-moment rates are \(1.75\), \(3.08\), and
\(4.57\), while on the deterministic spherical Voronoi-patch sequence they are
\(2.05\), \(2.52\), and \(3.62\).  The cubed-sphere \(p=3\) rate of \(4.57\) is
achieved via degree elevation of the polynomial basis by two orders, which
captures the leading rational correction from the contravariant Piola mapping.

As shown in Appendix~\ref{app:wachspress}, a componentwise Wachspress vector
interpolant is not a substitute for the harmonic mimetic reconstruction; only
vector-valued generalized barycentric constructions such as Whitney forms or
mixed VEM are viable future alternatives.

The added global target-edge projection changes the practical scope of the
method.  The local harmonic reconstruction remains the source-side engine for
exact edge-flux recovery and clipped-edge evaluation.  The new constrained
target solve then converts the raw directed target-edge field into a discrete
globally conforming \(H(\mathrm{div})\) flux field with exact target-cell
divergence constraints.  For divergence-free source reconstructions this means
that every target cell is also divergence-free to machine precision after the
projection, even on coarse nonmatching Voronoi meshes where the raw transfer can
show visible closure defects.

Taken together, the current repository now contains seven numerically distinct
but compatible layers: a verified low-order harmonic mimetic remap, a global
lowest-order target-edge conforming correction, a coupled multi-degree
target-edge conforming correction with proper edge-mass-weighted L\(^2\)
Legendre objective and an \(O(h^{p+1})\)-refining source-skeleton trace-jump
diagnostic, a unified higher-order split-moment extension that covers
\(p=1,2,3\) on planar polygons, structured spherical charts, and
deterministic spherical Voronoi patches, a Piola-consistent RT reconstruction
for quad cells that eliminates conditioning failures at \(p\ge2\), a VEM
H(div) projection with optional patch-based moment recovery that provides
topology-independent high-order transfer on arbitrary polygonal meshes
(triangles, quads, pentagons, hexagons, irregular n-gons), and a
Hodge-weighted spherical lift of that same VEM path with verified
\(O(h^{p+1})\) gradient-subspace convergence at \(p=1\) on cubed-sphere
meshes.  Furthermore, a practical single-flux production pipeline (one
scalar per source edge \(\to\) patch-based moment recovery \(\to\) VEM
projection) is verified to retain the gradient-subspace asymptotic rates
without any high-order seed data, with a forward-only diagnostic that
isolates source-side reconstruction quality from backward-leg
amplification.  The remaining gap to a full polygonal compatible finite
element framework is therefore no longer the absence of conservative
high-order evidence; it is the absence of a single globally conforming
cell-interior target space, the rotational-subspace upgrade for the
spherical VEM lift, and broader verification on mixed-topology and
production-scale spherical meshes.

\subsection*{Future Research Directions}
Building on the verified serial planar and spherical framework, the following
logical paths are proposed for future research:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Compatible barycentric H(div) spaces}: Study generalized
    Whitney-form bases built from Wachspress, mean-value, or harmonic
    coordinates \cite{GilletteRandBajaj2016}.  The acceptance criteria should
    be the same as in this manuscript: exact source-edge flux recovery,
    target-cell balance at \(5\times10^{-13}\), and sparse/direct transfer
    equivalence.  This is the mathematically appropriate way to revisit
    Wachspress ideas.
    \item \textbf{Spherical VEM rotational subspace}: The Hodge-weighted
    spherical lift of the VEM path (Section~\ref{sec:spherical-vem-lift})
    is implemented and verified to give optimal \(O(h^{p+1})\) convergence
    in the gradient subspace at \(p=1\) on cubed-sphere meshes, and to
    preserve cell-divergence conservation at machine roundoff at every
    tested order.  The remaining gap is the rotational subspace, which is
    capped at \(O(h^2)\) by the centroid-Hodge approximation in the
    rotational projection RHS and consequently dominates the asymptotic
    error for \(p\ge2\).  Closing this gap requires either Hodge-component
    cell-moment DOFs \(c_{xx}^{(\alpha)}=\int_{\hat K}\hat u_x x^a y^b
    h_{xx}\,d\xi\,d\eta\) (and analogous \(c_{xy}\), \(c_{yy}\)) or
    adoption of the SplitBasis degree-elevation rule for the spherical VEM
    polynomial basis.  Either path preserves the conservation guarantee.
    \item \textbf{High-order conservative remapping links}: Compare the
    directed edge-flux operator with high-order cell-average conservative
    remapping frameworks such as SCRIP-style remapping and TempestRemap
    \cite{Jones1999,UllrichTaylor2015,UllrichDevendranJohansen2016}.  This
    would clarify when edge-flux transfer and scalar conservative remapping can
    share overlap geometry and sparse operator infrastructure.
    \item \textbf{Higher-order spherical controls}: Replace the current
    piecewise-constant scalar control with a higher-order conservative
    spherical remapping baseline so that geometry-only and vector-space
    effects can be separated more sharply.
    \item \textbf{Spherical Robustness}: Extend beyond convex cells in one
    gnomonic hemisphere, add projection-singularity handling, and validate
    against larger realistic spherical meshes.
    \item \textbf{Broader spherical high-order validation}: Extend the present
    cubed-sphere and deterministic Voronoi-patch regressions to mixed
    Voronoi/cubed-sphere transfers, larger full-sphere meshes, and more
    challenging great-circle topologies, and measure whether the current
    structured-versus-unstructured order separation persists there.
    \item \textbf{Globally conforming target interiors}: Extend the present
    target-edge conforming projection to a single compatible target-cell
    interior field so that higher-order moment transfer and global
    \(H(\mathrm{div})\) conformity are enforced in one space rather than by a
    skeleton correction followed by cellwise reconstruction.
    \item \textbf{3D Polyhedral Support}: Extend the geometric intersection and
    interpolation logic to fully three-dimensional polyhedra.  This would
    require volumetric H(div) reconstruction, surface clipping, and flux
    balance over polyhedral intersection boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Integration}: Package the library into a modular component
    for larger simulation frameworks.  Distributed mesh loading, MPI halo
    exchange, and language bindings should remain downstream of the serial
    numerical invariants.
\end{itemize}

At that point the same overlap invariant, Eq.~\eqref{eq:overlap_conservation},
should remain the primary acceptance test for these generalized topologies.

\paragraph{Remaining limitations.}
The current spherical backend assumes convex great-circle cells within the
source-cell gnomonic hemisphere.  The global target-edge conforming projection
does not yet construct a single globally conforming cell-interior vector field.
The candidate search is deterministic all-pairs; production use requires spatial
acceleration.  MPI, distributed ownership, and parallel I/O are out of scope.

\section{Why Wachspress Coordinates Are Not a Drop-In Replacement}
\label{app:wachspress}

\begin{proposition}[Obstruction for componentwise Wachspress flux transfer]
Let \(K\) be a convex polygon with at least one non-affine Wachspress coordinate
\(\lambda_i\).  A componentwise vector interpolant
\[
  \mathbf{v}_W(\x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{c}_i\lambda_i(\x),
  \qquad \mathbf{c}_i\in\R^2,
\]
does not define an RT0-like edge-flux space: its divergence is generically
nonconstant in \(K\), and its normal trace is generically nonconstant along an
edge.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The divergence \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}_W = \sum_i \mathbf{c}_i\cdot\nabla\lambda_i\)
would be constant for every \(\mathbf{c}_i\) only if each \(\lambda_i\) were
affine.  Wachspress coordinates are affine only on simplices; for general \(n>3\)
polygons they are rational and non-affine
\cite{Wachspress1975,FloaterHormannKos2006}.  On an edge \(e=[x_j,x_{j+1}]\),
the normal trace
\(\mathbf{v}_W\cdot\n_e = (\mathbf{c}_j\cdot\n_e)\lambda_j +
(\mathbf{c}_{j+1}\cdot\n_e)\lambda_{j+1}\) is constant only if the two endpoint
normal components are equal.  A single integrated edge flux does not enforce
that pointwise equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Generalized Whitney forms can produce H(div) spaces on convex polytopes
\cite{GilletteRandBajaj2016} and should be studied as a new compatible finite
element discretization with its own normal-trace basis, mass matrix, and
quadrature rules.
\end{remark}
